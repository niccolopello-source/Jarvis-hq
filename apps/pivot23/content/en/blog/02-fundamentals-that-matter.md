# The fundamentals that matter: how a complete player is built

In basketball people talk a lot about athleticism, dunks and deep threes. Yet anyone who has spent time in a gym knows that long careers are built on something less spectacular: fundamentals. In this article we look at the basic skills that make a complete player, why they're trained for a lifetime, and how we chose to represent them in PIVOT 23 without turning them into a list of numbers.

## Shooting: repetition and mechanics

Shooting is the most visible fundamental and also the one that demands the most patience. Good mechanics start with balance: stable feet, elbow, wrist and rim lined up, and the same release every time. Great shooters repeat the same motion thousands of times because the goal is for the body, under pressure and when tired, to do exactly what it did in practice.

In modern basketball the three-point shot has changed how the floor is used. A player who can shoot forces the defense to spread out and creates space for teammates, even when he doesn't touch the ball. That's one reason why big men today also work a lot on their shot.

## Ball handling: control, not show

Dribbling is first of all about not losing the ball and getting it where it needs to go. Basic drills teach you to dribble with both hands, keep your head up and protect the ball with your body. Only after that come changes of direction and more creative moves.

A good ball handler isn't the one with the most tricks, but the one who makes better decisions with the ball in his hands. Knowing how to attack an advantage, stop when needed and pass at the right moment is worth more than any highlight move.

## Passing: seeing it before others do

Passing is the fundamental most tied to basketball IQ. It requires technique, but above all vision: understanding where a teammate will be a moment before he gets there. Great passers read help defense, know when a simple pass beats a risky one, and make their teammates better.

## Defense: feet, position, communication

Defense is often underrated by viewers and highly valued by coaches. It's built on footwork, positioning relative to the opponent and the basket, and communication with teammates. A smart defender doesn't just guard his man: he helps, recovers, calls out screens and anticipates what the offense wants to do.

## Rebounding: timing and desire

Grabbing a rebound isn't only about height. Boxing out matters, meaning putting your body between your opponent and the basket, and so does the timing to read the ball's flight. Many coaches say rebounding is mostly a matter of determination.

## Basketball IQ

Then there's a quality that ties all the others together: understanding the game. Knowing when to speed up and when to slow down, which shot to take, how to exploit a favorable matchup. It's the quality that often lets veterans stay useful even when their body is no longer what it was at twenty.

## Why fundamentals never end

One of the most interesting things about fundamentals is that you never stop improving them. Many professional players work every summer on a specific aspect, such as a new post move or a quicker release. A career is also the story of how an athlete adds tools to his game and adapts his style as time goes by.

## How we represent them in PIVOT 23

In PIVOT 23 a player has eight visible attributes inspired by these fundamentals and by the body: shooting, ball handling, passing, defense, rebounding, athleticism, strength and basketball IQ. Alongside them there are a few hidden traits, tied to character, that are only revealed at the end of a career. We wanted them to be easy to understand even for non-experts, while still telling the story of a profile: the pure shooter, the tireless defender, the floor general who sees everything.

We don't reveal how these qualities interact inside the game engine, because we'd rather players discover it career after career. We can say, though, that balance matters and that a player with a clear identity has a more interesting story to tell.

## In short

Fundamentals are the foundation of every career, in real life and in the game. Shooting, ball handling, passing, defense, rebounding and basketball IQ are trained for years and change over time. Understanding them helps you watch basketball with different eyes, and build more believable players when you play.
